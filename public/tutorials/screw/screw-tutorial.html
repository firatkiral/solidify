Modeling a Threaded Hex Screw

A step-by-step tutorial for Solidify, from a hexagon sketch to a cut thread

ResultHex-head screw with a chamfered head, chamfered tip and a 60° thread
WorkflowThe usual FreeCAD/Fusion screw tutorial: extrude, intersect, sweep a profile along a helix, subtract
SizeHead 2 units across corners, shank radius 0.45 units, length 3 units, pitch 0.25 units

Tools used

Every tool below is in the right-hand toolbar or the bottom toolbar, and most have a keyboard shortcut. The shortcuts in a command only work while the mouse is over the viewport.

General keys: Enter or OK confirms a command, Esc cancels it, and Ctrl+Z undoes. Values are typed into the dialog at the bottom left. Lengths are in scene units. The extrude "Race" angle is in radians (−0.52 rad = −30°).

Overview

  1. Draw a hexagon and extrude it into the head.
  2. Round the head's corners: intersect it with a 30° cone.
  3. Extrude the shank from the underside of the head and join it.
  4. Chamfer the tip.
  5. Draw a helix around the shank with the Spiral tool.
  6. Draw a 60° triangle at the start of the helix.
  7. Sweep the triangle along the helix into a coil.
  8. Subtract the coil from the screw to cut the thread.
1

Draw the hexagon

Regular polygon
  1. In the right-hand toolbar, click Regular polygon (the hexagon icon).
  2. Click the origin to place the center.
  3. Move the mouse out along the red X axis. The polygon starts with 5 sides: hold Shift and scroll the wheel up once to get 6.
  4. Click 1 unit from the center. A closed curve becomes a region, which can be extruded.
2

Extrude the head

Extrude · E
  1. Click inside the hexagon to select the region.
  2. Press E, or click Extrude in the bottom toolbar.
  3. In the dialog, set the first Distance to 0.70 (upwards), then click OK.
3

Round the head's corners

Center and radius circle · Shift+WExtrude with angleBoolean · Q → E (Intersect)

Real bolt heads have their corners trimmed by a 30° cone, which leaves curved arcs on each flat. We build that cone and keep only the part of the head inside it.

  1. Press Shift+W. Click the center of the top face, then click about 0.89 units out, just beyond the middle of the flats.
  2. Select only this circle region and press E. Set Distance to 0.00 in the first box and 0.70 in the second, so it goes down through the head. Set the second Race (angle) box to −0.52 rad (−30°) so the cone widens downwards. Click OK, which creates a separate cone body.
  3. Select the head, then Shift-click the cone. Press Q (Boolean) and then E for Intersect; the preview turns green. Click OK.
4

Extrude the shank

Circle · Shift+WExtrude · E → Q (Union)
  1. Orbit the view (middle-mouse drag) until you look at the underside of the head.
  2. Press Shift+W, click the center of the bottom face and draw a circle of radius 0.45.
  3. Select the circle region and Shift-click the head, then press E.
  4. Drag the arrow a little (or press D, move and click). This switches on the union, difference, intersect and new-body keys.
  5. Set Distance to 0.00 in the first box and 3.00 in the second, then press Q for Union and click OK.
When an extrusion touches a body, it starts out as a cut (red preview). Press Q to join it to the body instead.
5

Chamfer the tip

Fillet / chamfer · B
  1. Select the circular edge at the bottom of the shank (edge selection mode: 2).
  2. Press B. Type −0.12 into both Distance boxes; a negative distance makes a chamfer instead of a fillet.
  3. Click OK.
6

Draw the helix

Hide · HXZ construction planeSpiral

The thread follows a helix around the shank. Draw it in a front view on the XZ plane, so the clicks land exactly on the shank's axis.

  1. Select the screw and press H to hide it, so your clicks don't snap to its faces.
  2. Click XZ in the right-hand panel and look from the front.
  3. Click Spiral in the right-hand toolbar.
  4. Click the axis start just below the tip at (0, −3.25), then the axis end straight above it at (0, −0.45); the Z-axis snap keeps it vertical. Finally click the radius point to the right (+X).
  5. In the dialog set Step (the number of turns) to 11.20 and Radius to 0.4536, the shank radius. That gives a pitch of 2.8 / 11.2 = 0.25. Press Enter.
The helix starts below the tip so the thread runs out cleanly at the end. It stops 0.45 below the head, leaving a plain section under the head.
7

Draw the thread profile

Line · Shift+A

The thread profile is a 60° triangle in the XZ plane at the helix's start point. Its base sits 0.05 outside the shank and its tip points 0.13 into it.

  1. Select the helix and press H, so the line clicks don't snap onto it. Zoom in on the bottom of the axis.
  2. Press Shift+A (Line) and click (0.50, −3.35), (0.50, −3.15) and (0.32, −3.25).
  3. Click the first point again: the Closed snap closes the triangle, and it becomes a region.
8

Sweep the profile along the helix

Unhide (eye icon)Sweep · Alt+E
  1. Show the screw and the helix again with their eye icons in the Scene list.
  2. Select the triangle region, then Shift-click the helix.
  3. Press Alt+E (Sweep). Keep Mode: Preserve; the profile then turns with the helix and stays in the axial plane. Press Enter.
9

Cut the thread

Boolean · Q → W (Difference)
  1. Select the screw, then Shift-click the coil.
  2. Press Q (Boolean), then W for Difference; the coil turns red. Click OK.
  3. Hide the sketch curves (select them in the Scene list and press H).
✓

Finished screw

Variations

  • Finer or coarser thread: change the Spiral's Step (number of turns) and scale the triangle to match the pitch.
  • Tapered (wood-screw) thread: give the Spiral an Angle; its radius then changes along the axis.
  • Socket head: draw a hexagon on the top face and extrude it down as a cut (difference) instead of rounding the corners.
